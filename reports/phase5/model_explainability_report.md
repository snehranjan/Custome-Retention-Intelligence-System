## 1. Mathematical Framework for Logistic Regression Explainability

In a binary Logistic Regression framework predicting customer churn ($y=1$), the model computes the linear score $\eta$ for customer $i$ as:
$$\eta_i = \beta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j x_{ij}$$
The predicted posterior probability $p_i = P(y_i = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ is given by the logistic sigmoid link function:
$$p_i = \sigma(\eta_i) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\eta_i}}$$
The model's log-odds (logit) of churn is strictly linear in the transformed features:
$$\text{logit}(p_i) = \ln\left(\frac{p_i}{1 - p_i}\right) = \beta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j x_{ij}$$
- **Fitted Intercept ($\beta_0$):** `-0.0795`. This represents the baseline log-odds of churn when all standardized continuous features are at their sample means (0) and all one-hot categorical features are at their zero states.
- **Fitted Regularization:** $C = 0.01$ applies strong $L_2$ shrinkage (ridge penalty $\frac{1}{2C} \sum \beta_j^2$), which shrinks coefficients toward zero to guard against multi-collinearity and high variance.

### 1.1 Interpretation of Fitted Coefficients & $\exp(\beta)$
In binary Logistic Regression, the exponentiated coefficient is computed as $\exp(\beta_j)$.
Proper statistical interpretation depends strictly on the feature type and encoding strategy:

1. **Continuous Standardized Features:** All numerical features were standardized using `StandardScaler` ($z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$). For these features, each coefficient $\beta_j$ represents the change in log-odds of churn per **one standard-deviation increase ($+1\sigma$)**, holding all other independent features constant. Here, $\exp(\beta_j)$ represents the **Odds Ratio ($\text{OR}$)**: a $+1\sigma$ increase multiplies the churn odds ($\frac{p}{1-p}$) by $\exp(\beta_j)$, ceteris paribus.
2. **One-Hot Categorical Features (`drop=None`):** The pipeline's `OneHotEncoder` was configured with `drop=None` and `handle_unknown="ignore"`, retaining indicator columns for all observed categories (e.g., all 5 RFM clusters, all cities, and all states).
   - **$L_2$ Regularization & Identifiability:** L2 regularization penalizes the categorical coefficients and helps resolve the non-identifiability associated with redundant one-hot indicators. Under the fitted model's regularized objective, this supports a unique coefficient solution. It does not imply that the resulting coefficients are independent of the encoding or regularization strength, nor does it guarantee good numerical conditioning.
   - **Regularized Linear Score Parameters:** Categorical coefficients $\beta_j$ are regularized parameters that contribute $\beta_j \cdot x_j$ directly to the model's linear prediction score $\eta$. They must **not** be described as direct comparisons against an omitted reference category.
   - **Invalidity of Single-Indicator Ceteris Paribus Transitions:** Changing one category indicator while holding all other category indicators within the same feature constant is **not** a valid counterfactual or *ceteris paribus* transition. Because categories within a group are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive ($\sum x_k = 1$), activating one indicator necessarily requires deactivating another.
   - **Shrinkage & Absence of Inherent Bias Elimination:** Full one-hot encoding does not inherently eliminate reference-category bias. The extent of shrinkage depends on the design matrix, feature correlations, category frequencies, and regularization strength, penalizing coefficient magnitudes toward zero under the regularized loss objective.
   - **Exponentiated Parameter ($\exp(\beta)$):** For categorical indicators, $\exp(\beta_j)$ represents the mathematical exponentiation of the fitted parameter $\beta_j$, not a standalone odds ratio relative to a baseline reference category under this encoding.
3. **Missingness Indicators:** Binary indicators generated by `SimpleImputer(add_indicator=True)` represent whether the underlying historical feature was unrecorded ($x_j \in \{0, 1\}$). The coefficient represents the additive contribution to the linear score when the metric was missing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Critical Statistical Disclaimer on Causation:** Logistic Regression coefficients quantify **statistical associations (conditional dependencies)** learned from the data, **NOT causal effects**. A positive coefficient indicates that customers with higher values of this feature have higher odds of churning in the training distribution; it does *not* imply that changing this feature will cause churn.

## 5. Academic Discussion for Viva Voce

### Q1: Why did you report odds ratios rather than feature percentage probabilities?
> **Answer:** In Logistic Regression, the model is linear in the log-odds space, not in probability space. Because the sigmoid transformation is non-linear, the change in probability associated with a one-unit change in a feature depends on the starting probability $\Delta p = \sigma(\eta + \beta) - \sigma(\eta)$. Odds Ratios ($\text{OR} = \exp(\beta)$) are constant multiplicative factors on the *odds* ratio, making them mathematically precise and invariant across baseline risk levels.

### Q2: Why are continuous features interpreted per one standard deviation?
> **Answer:** During preprocessing, all continuous features were passed through `StandardScaler()`, subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation ($z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$). Consequently, a one-unit increase in the transformed feature corresponds exactly to a one standard-deviation increase in the original e-commerce metric.

### Q3: Can marketing teams assume that offering discounts will prevent churn because of negative coefficients on discounts?
> **Answer:** No. That would confuse correlation with causation. Observational data shows that customers who received discounts engaged more, but unobserved confounding (e.g., promotional eligibility, customer willingness to buy) may drive both discount receipt and retention. Controlled A/B testing would be necessary to establish causal efficacy.

### Q4: Why did the OneHotEncoder retain all categories without dropping a reference level (`drop=None`)?
> **Answer:** In unregularized linear models, omitting a reference category is mathematically required to prevent the 'dummy variable trap' (exact multicollinearity where indicator columns sum to 1). In regularized Logistic Regression, however, L2 regularization penalizes the categorical coefficients and helps resolve the non-identifiability associated with redundant one-hot indicators. Under the fitted model's regularized objective, this supports a unique coefficient solution. It does not imply that the resulting coefficients are independent of the encoding or regularization strength, nor does it guarantee good numerical conditioning. Full one-hot encoding does not inherently eliminate reference-category bias; rather, shrinkage depends on the design matrix, feature correlations, category frequencies, and regularization strength. Categorical coefficients are regularized linear parameters contributing $\beta_j \cdot x_j$ to the prediction score, not comparisons against an omitted baseline category. Crucially, changing one category indicator while holding all other category indicators within the same feature constant is not a valid counterfactual or *ceteris paribus* transition, because mutually exclusive categories must sum to 1. Consequently, $\exp(\beta_j)$ represents the mathematical exponentiation of parameter $\beta_j$, not a standalone odds ratio relative to an omitted baseline.
